Peek: preview links without opening a tab

Shift+click a link to open it in a card over the page you’re reading. Close it, or keep it as a tab without reloading.

Most links you click, you only need to glance at. Peek lets you read the page behind a link without leaving the one you're on, with no new tab to close afterwards.

How it works

  • Shift+click a link. The card grows out of the link while the window dims.
  • Read it, scroll it, use it like any page. It has the ad blocker, popup blocker and your password manager.
  • Esc, ⌘W, a click on the dimmed backdrop or the Close button let it go.
  • Open as tab keeps it: the card becomes a tab next to the page it came from, without reloading, and the visit goes into history.
  • Open in split view moves the card into the right half, with the page you were on beside it.

Careful by design

  • Only you can open one. A page can't open a Peek with a script, even during a click.
  • You can see where you are. A label above the card shows the page's site, so a page in a Peek can't pass for the one under it. A page the address bar would warn about shows its warning there.
  • No permission prompts. A page in a Peek can't ask for the camera, location or notifications, sign in to a server, open another app or go fullscreen until you open it as a tab.

If you're coming from Arc

Peek works the same way as in Arc: Shift+click, and the page appears over the one you're reading. See Little Browser as an Arc alternative, including how to bring your Arc spaces and tabs with you.

Questions

What is Peek?

A link preview. Shift+click a link and the page opens in a card over the one you’re reading, with the rest of the window dimmed. Close it and you’re back where you were.

How do I open a Peek?

Hold Shift and click a link, including links that would open a new tab. A page can’t open a Peek by itself, only your own click does.

Can I turn a Peek into a tab?

Yes: the Open as tab button expands it into a tab beside the page it came from, without reloading it. Open in split view puts it in the right half instead.

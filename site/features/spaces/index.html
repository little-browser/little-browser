Features / Spaces

Spaces: a browser tab sidebar for each part of your life

Personal, work, a hobby: each space has its own tabs, name and colour, and comes back after a restart.

A space is a place for one part of your life, with its own tabs, its own name and a colour that tints the sidebar. Switch spaces and the sidebar slides over to a different set of tabs, and the page behind it with them.

What a space holds

  • Pinned and today tabs. Pinned tabs stay put; today's tabs are the ones you're working through. Tab groups work in both.
  • A name, an emoji icon and a colour. Make a new one from the + at the bottom of the sidebar or the command bar.
  • A profile, when you want one. Give a space its own profile to keep its logins, cookies and history apart, so work and personal accounts never meet. A small lock badge shows when a space isn't in your default profile.
  • Its tabs across restarts. Every window comes back on its own space. Tabs in spaces you aren't using go to sleep after a while to save memory, and wake when you click them.

Switching

  • ⌃ 1…9, or ⌃ ⌥ ← / →
  • The space dots at the bottom of the sidebar
  • A two-finger sideways swipe on the sidebar. On the page itself the swipe still goes back and forward.
  • Right-click a tab, then Move to space.

If you're coming from Arc

Spaces work the way you'd expect from Arc, and Little Browser imports your Arc spaces along with their tabs. See Little Browser as an Arc alternative.

Questions

What is a space?

A set of pinned tabs and today’s tabs with a name, an icon and a colour. Use one for each part of your life: personal, work, a hobby.

How do I switch spaces?

Press Ctrl+1…9, use the dots at the bottom of the sidebar, or swipe sideways with two fingers on the sidebar.

Are spaces the same as profiles?

No. A profile is one set of logins, cookies and history. Every space uses a profile, and several spaces can share one. Everyone starts with a single profile, so you only meet the idea if you want logins kept apart.

Can I bring my Arc spaces?

Yes. The import reads Arc’s spaces, with their pinned tabs, open tabs and the profiles behind them.

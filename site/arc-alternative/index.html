Coming from Arc?

An Arc alternative for Mac

Arc is being discontinued. Little Browser is a small, private, open-source browser with the parts of Arc many people loved: the sidebar, spaces and Peek.

Arc, the sidebar browser from The Browser Company, is being shut down. New accounts closed on October 22, 2026, and on December 22, 2026 Arc stops working on macOS and Windows; accounts are then deactivated. The team's focus has moved to their newer browser, Dia. The details are in Arc’s own announcement.

Little Browser started from the same idea Arc did: tabs on the side, a space for each part of your life, and the page getting the window. It was inspired by Arc, and it is an independent, open-source browser, so it can't be switched off by someone else's roadmap.

What carries over from Arc

In ArcIn Little Browser
Sidebar with vertical tabsThe same layout, built on Chromium’s own vertical tabs. It collapses to icons, or hides and slides in over the page.
SpacesSpaces, each with its own tabs, icon and colour. Switch with ⌃ 1…9 or a two-finger swipe on the sidebar. A space can use its own profile to keep logins apart.
Pinned tabs and FavoritesPinned tabs, per space.
FoldersTab groups: named, collapsible runs of tabs, among today’s tabs or the pinned ones.
Peek (Shift+click a link)Peek: the same gesture. Open it as a tab without a reload, or in split view.
Little ArcThe little window: a small window with no tabs, for a link from a chat or a quick look-up. ⌥ ⌘ N.
Command barThe command bar: ⌘ T and ⌘ L search tabs, bookmarks, history and commands from one place.
Split viewChromium’s split view, two tabs side by side.
Chrome extensionsChrome Web Store extensions (Manifest V3). Your Arc extensions are offered for reinstall when you import.

Moving your tabs, spaces and bookmarks

Little Browser offers to import on first run, and later from the command bar (“Import from another browser”). From Arc it brings your spaces (and the profiles behind them), open tabs, pinned tabs and Favourites, bookmarks, history, search shortcuts and extensions. You choose what comes along. Staying signed in is opt-in, and passwords stay with your password manager. Arc can stay open while you import.

Arc’s own settings can also save your tabs as a list of links (Arc › Settings › Advanced › Export Data), which is worth doing before December 22.

What's different

  • No account, no telemetry. Your history, tabs and passwords stay on your device. See the privacy policy.
  • Open source. The code is on GitHub under MPL-2.0.
  • Built-in ad and tracker blocking, and stricter defaults than stock Chromium.
  • No AI features. It also has no Boosts or Easels.
  • Early. It is a beta, for Macs with Apple silicon only. Windows, Linux and mobile versions don't exist yet, and sync between devices is designed but not built.

Questions

Is Arc being discontinued?

Yes. Its makers, The Browser Company, are sunsetting Arc: new accounts stopped on October 22, 2026, and on December 22, 2026 Arc stops working on macOS and Windows. See Arc’s own announcement for the timeline.

What is the best Arc alternative for Mac?

It depends what you liked about Arc. If it was the sidebar, spaces and Peek, without an account or AI, Little Browser is built for that. It is a beta for Macs with Apple silicon, open source, and keeps your data on your device.

Can I import my Arc spaces and tabs?

Yes. Little Browser’s import reads Arc’s spaces, profiles, open tabs, pinned tabs and Favourites, plus bookmarks, history, search shortcuts and extensions. Import while Arc is still installed, which is before December 22, 2026.

Does Little Browser have Peek?

Yes. Shift+click a link to open it in a card over the page you’re reading, as in Arc. More about Peek.

Does Little Browser need an account?

No. There’s no account, no sign-in and no telemetry. Sync between devices is designed but not built yet.

What isn’t in Little Browser?

Arc’s Boosts, Easels and AI features. It also runs only on Macs with Apple silicon for now; Windows, Linux, iPhone and Android versions are not available yet.
